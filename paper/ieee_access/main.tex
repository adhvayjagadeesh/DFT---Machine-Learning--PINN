\begin{abstract}
Hybrid architectures that place a neural residual corrector on top of a gradient-boosted prior are widely reported to improve tabular property prediction in materials informatics. We test that claim for HSE06 band-gap prediction of rectangular-lattice two-dimensional materials under an evaluation protocol built to close the leakage pathways that inflate such comparisons: composition-grouped cross-validation, fold-local encoding and scaling, out-of-fold construction of the tree prior, a held-out test set carved before model selection, and significance testing at the fold level with the Nadeau--Bengio variance correction. On 1{,}169 C2DB materials assessed over 25 fold estimates from repeated grouped cross-validation, the physics-informed hybrid does \emph{not} improve on its own gradient-boosting prior ($R^2 = 0.810 \pm 0.047$ versus $0.823 \pm 0.043$; superior on 12 of 25 fold estimates; $p = 0.27$). A component ablation in which every configuration is trained shows that removing the structural coupling layer, the boundary penalty, or the anisotropy penalty leaves accuracy unchanged or slightly improved, and that the tree prior alone outperforms the complete framework. Reverting to an in-sample prior construction \emph{raises} measured accuracy, identifying the mechanism: a corrector trained against in-sample predictions learns to emit approximately zero and degenerates into a pass-through of the tree. Two positive results survive. Neural models outperform every tree ensemble once composition-derived descriptors replace formula indicator variables, inverting the usual tabular-data expectation; and heterogeneous stacked generalisation improves cross-validated $R^2$ from $0.823$ to $0.877$ and MAE from $0.464$ to $0.363$ eV, superior on 25 of 25 fold estimates ($p < 0.001$). A drop-one ensemble ablation shows the two neural members carry the ensemble while the tree members are redundant. We release the complete pipeline so that both the negative and the positive result reproduce end to end.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
\PARstart{A}{ccurate} prediction of electronic band gaps ($E_g$) is a foundational objective in computational materials design. The magnitude and nature of the band gap govern whether a low-dimensional material acts as a metal, semiconductor, or insulator, dictate optical absorption profiles, and determine candidate suitability for field-effect transistors \cite{fiori2014electronics}, valleytronic architectures \cite{schaibley2016valleytronics,xiao2012coupled}, and photocatalytic water splitting \cite{singh2015computational}. Density functional theory with hybrid functionals such as HSE06 \cite{heyd2003hybrid} offers excellent accuracy at computational scaling that renders exhaustive high-throughput screening impractical, motivating fast surrogate estimators.

For heterogeneous tabular materials data, decision-tree ensembles \cite{breiman2001random,friedman2001greedy} frequently match or exceed deep architectures \cite{shwartzziv2022tabular,grinsztajn2022why}, although the conditions under which this holds are narrower than is often assumed \cite{mcelfresh2023when}. A natural response is the hybrid residual design: a tree supplies a prior estimate and a neural network learns a correction, optionally regularised by physical constraints in the manner of physics-informed neural networks \cite{raissi2019physics,karniadakis2021physics}. Such designs are attractive because they appear to combine the sample efficiency of ensembles with the smoothness of neural function approximation, and because anisotropic two-dimensional systems --- where in-plane lattice vectors are unequal and strain couples strongly to the electronic structure \cite{rodin2014strain,li2014black,xia2014rediscovering} --- are precisely the setting in which axis-aligned tree partitions appear least suited.

An earlier version of this work reported such a result: a gradient-boosted prior coupled to a quantile residual head with an engineered structural layer, improving pooled $R^2$ from $0.7323$ to $0.7431$ with a nominally significant $p$-value. This article reports what happened when that pipeline was re-evaluated under controls for data leakage. The improvement did not survive, and the reason is instructive rather than incidental. Leakage of exactly the kinds we identify has been argued to be a principal driver of irreproducibility across machine-learning-based science \cite{kapoor2023leakage}.

Our contribution is threefold.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A leakage-controlled evaluation protocol} for tabular materials regression, closing six specific pathways: identifier-like features, whole-dataset encoding, in-sample construction of stacked priors, polymorphs straddling folds, absent held-out data, and significance tests applied at the sample rather than the fold level.

\item \textbf{A negative result with an identified mechanism.} Under that protocol the physics-informed hybrid does not improve on its own prior, and no physics-motivated component contributes measurably. We show that the earlier apparent gain arose because an in-sample prior trains the corrector to be inert.

\item \textbf{A positive alternative.} Heterogeneous stacked generalisation \cite{wolpert1992stacked,breiman1996stacked} yields a large and consistent improvement on the same data and protocol, and a drop-one ablation identifies which members are responsible.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Dataset and Target Definition}
The learning domain comprises 1{,}169 rectangular-lattice (\textit{op}) two-dimensional monolayer structures drawn from the Computational 2D Materials Database \cite{haastrup2018c2db} that carry a band gap computed with the HSE06 screened hybrid functional \cite{heyd2003hybrid}. The target $y \in \mathbb{R}^+$ spans $0.001$--$8.85$ eV with mean $2.60$ eV.

An earlier revision of this work reported 930 materials. That figure resulted from applying a blanket missing-value filter across all columns, which discarded 239 labelled entries because a single descriptor --- total magnetic moment --- was absent. We instead impute that column from training-fold medians and carry an explicit missingness indicator, recovering the full labelled set.

We emphasise one property of the feature space that governs comparability with prior work: it contains \emph{no} previously computed band gap of any level of theory. Several strong published results on C2DB supply a semi-local gap (for example a PBE gap without spin--orbit coupling) as an input feature \cite{zhang2021bandgap}, which converts the task into $\Delta$-learning between functionals and is substantially easier. The task addressed here is prediction of HSE06 gaps with no prior electronic-structure calculation on the target material.

\subsection{Descriptors and Fold-Local Encoding}
Each material is represented by seven continuous C2DB descriptors (energy above hull, heat of formation, thickness, total energy, unit-cell area, vacuum level, total magnetic moment), three nominal descriptors (magnetic state, layer group, stoichiometry prototype), an atom count parsed with \textit{pymatgen} \cite{ong2013pymatgen}, a packing-density proxy
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\mathrm{cell}} = \frac{N_{\mathrm{atoms}}}{A_{\mathrm{cell}}},
\label{eq:density}
\end{equation}
and 120 Magpie elemental-property statistics \cite{ward2016general} computed with \textit{matminer} \cite{ward2018matminer}.

The Magpie statistics replace the raw chemical formula. The earlier pipeline one-hot encoded the formula string into 1{,}077 indicator columns, of which 825 occurred in exactly one material. Such columns are row identifiers rather than descriptors: a formula appearing only in a validation fold contributes an all-zero block and cannot support generalisation.

Categorical vocabularies, imputation medians, and $z$-score statistics
\begin{equation}
\tilde{x}_{ij} = \frac{x_{ij} - \mu_j}{\sigma_j}
\label{eq:zscore}
\end{equation}
are estimated on the training partition only. The earlier pipeline applied one-hot encoding to the full table before cross-validation, exposing the category vocabulary of every validation fold.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
Four further controls define the protocol.

\textit{Composition grouping.} 176 labelled materials share a chemical formula with at least one other entry. These are polymorphs, distinguished by layer group, whose band gaps differ by up to $2.92$ eV. Because composition-derived descriptors are near-identical within such a pair, an ungrouped split permits a model to memorise one polymorph and be scored on another. All splits are therefore grouped on chemical formula.

\textit{Out-of-fold priors.} The hybrid's correction head is trained against out-of-fold predictions from an inner five-fold split rather than in-sample predictions of the fitted tree, following standard stacked generalisation \cite{wolpert1992stacked}. Section~\ref{sec:ablation} shows this single change accounts for most of the previously reported gain.

\textit{Held-out test set.} 15\% of materials (179 entries), split by formula group, are withheld before any model selection.

\textit{Repeated cross-validation and corrected inference.} We report five repeats of grouped five-fold cross-validation, giving 25 fold estimates; small numbers of folds yield error bars too wide to separate competing models \cite{varoquaux2018crossvalidation}. Comparisons use the Nadeau--Bengio variance-corrected paired $t$-test \cite{nadeau2003inference}, which accounts for the positive correlation induced by overlapping training sets. We note that a paired test applied to per-sample squared errors --- as used in the earlier revision --- treats non-independent quantities as independent observations of model performance and overstates the effective sample size by roughly two orders of magnitude.

\subsection{Models}
Baselines are random forest (RF), gradient boosting (GBR), support vector regression (SVR) \cite{drucker1996support}, a deep multilayer perceptron (MLP) with SiLU activations \cite{elfwing2018sigmoid,ramachandran2017searching} and batch normalisation \cite{ioffe2015batch}, and a physics-informed network (PINN) sharing the MLP backbone with an added boundary penalty
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phy}} = \mathrm{ReLU}(-\hat{y}) + 0.1\left(e^{\mathrm{ReLU}(0.05-\hat{y})}-1\right),
\label{eq:phy}
\end{equation}
which discourages physically impossible negative gaps. All baselines receive an identical randomised hyperparameter search budget within each training fold; the earlier comparison tuned the hybrid by hand while leaving baselines at library defaults.

The hybrid forms the augmented payload
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{hybrid}} = \left[\mathbf{x},\; \mathbf{x}\odot\hat{y}_{\mathrm{prior}},\; \hat{y}_{\mathrm{prior}}\right]
\label{eq:payload}
\end{equation}
and adds a learned correction to the prior. A structural coupling layer builds
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{s} = \left[g_1 g_2,\; g_1^2-g_2^2,\; \sqrt{g_1^2+g_2^2+\epsilon},\; g_1,\; g_2\right]
\label{eq:strain}
\end{equation}
from two geometric channels $g_1, g_2$ and projects it back onto the feature map through a small multilayer block. Three heads predict quantiles at $\tau \in \{0.25, 0.50, 0.75\}$ \cite{koenker1978regression} alongside a point residual head.

One correction to the earlier implementation is material to every physical claim made about this layer. The channels were selected by array position (indices 0 and 1), which in the assembled feature matrix were energy above hull and heat of formation --- two formation energies, not geometric quantities. Channels are now resolved by name. We further note that the available C2DB export contains no in-plane lattice vectors $a$ and $b$; the geometric channels are unit-cell area and thickness. We therefore describe Eq.~\eqref{eq:strain} as a soft geometric inductive bias and make no claim that it is derived from elasticity theory.

The hybrid does not improve on its prior: it is superior on 12 of 25 fold estimates, and the corrected test does not reject the null ($p = 0.27$). On the held-out test set the gap is larger and in the same direction ($R^2 = 0.775$ against $0.803$ for GBR).

Two baselines invert the expected ordering. The MLP and the PINN both outperform every tree ensemble. With identifier-like features removed and composition statistics supplied, this task falls outside the regime in which boosted trees dominate neural networks \cite{mcelfresh2023when}, which removes the premise motivating a tree prior in the first place.

No physics-motivated component contributes positively. Removing the structural layer, the boundary penalty, or the anisotropy penalty leaves performance unchanged to within noise, and the tree prior alone is the strongest configuration in the table.

The second row is diagnostic. Reverting to the leaky in-sample prior \emph{raises} measured $R^2$. With a prior that appears near-perfect on training rows, the correction head has almost no residual signal to fit; it learns to emit approximately zero and degenerates into a pass-through of the tree. The apparent benefit of the original architecture was therefore substantially an artefact of the head being trained to do nothing, and the ablation recovers that artefact on demand.

\subsection{Uncertainty Calibration}
The architecture predicts quantiles, and earlier revisions described these as uncertainty estimates. The nominal $q_{0.25}$--$q_{0.75}$ interval should cover 50\% of outcomes; measured empirical coverage is $17.2\%$ at a mean interval width of $2.47$ eV (Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration}). The intervals are simultaneously wide and badly miscalibrated. We therefore withdraw any uncertainty claim for this architecture; a claim of that kind requires scoring the predictive distribution with a proper rule \cite{gneiting2007probabilistic}.

\subsection{Stacked Generalisation}
\label{sec:stacking}
A ridge meta-learner over out-of-fold predictions of all five base models attains $R^2 = 0.877 \pm 0.038$ and MAE $= 0.363$ eV, superior to GBR on all 25 fold estimates ($p < 0.001$). A non-negative least-squares (NNLS) variant performs equivalently and is directly interpretable.

Fig.~\ref{fig:contributions}(a) shows mean NNLS weights: MLP $0.343$, PINN $0.297$, GBR $0.238$, RF $0.142$, SVR $0.000$. Weights alone, however, do not establish that any member contributes, because collinear members split weight between them. Fig.~\ref{fig:contributions}(b) reports the drop-one ablation that does test it, over 15 fold estimates. Removing the PINN incurs the largest penalty ($\Delta\mathrm{MSE} = +0.0080$ eV$^2$, worse on 9/15 folds, $p = 0.016$), followed by the MLP ($+0.0073$, 10/15, $p = 0.051$) and the random forest ($+0.0035$, 10/15, $p = 0.008$). Removing GBR or SVR changes nothing measurable ($p = 0.39$ and $p = 0.11$).

\centerline{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig2_ensemble_contributions.png}}

The mechanism is error diversity rather than individual strength. \subsection{Held-Out Test Set}
On the 179 withheld materials the ordering of Table~\ref{tab:benchmark} is broadly reproduced: stacked ridge $R^2 = 0.856$, standalone PINN $0.855$, stacked NNLS $0.852$, MLP $0.849$, RF $0.809$, GBR $0.803$, SVR $0.726$, hybrid $0.775$.

We draw attention to one honest limitation of this result. On the held-out set the stacked ensemble and the standalone PINN are indistinguishable ($0.856$ against $0.855$). The clear separation between them observed in cross-validation (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark}) does not reproduce on a single split of 179 materials, which is small enough that its error bar comfortably spans the difference. The defensible claim is therefore that stacking reliably beats the \emph{tree} baselines, and that it is at least as good as the best single neural model; a claim that stacking beats the best single model outright would require a larger held-out set than this dataset affords.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why Residual Correction Fails Here}
Once the prior is constructed out of fold, its residuals on unseen materials are dominated by irreducible noise rather than by recoverable structure. A corrector fitted to such residuals adds variance without removing bias. The effect is not specific to materials data: residual-boosting correctors are known to produce negative gains when the residual signal is noise-dominated \cite{airlangga2025hybrid}.

We tested the natural remedy. A shrinkage gate constrains the correction by $\sigma(\cdot) \in (0,1)$ and is initialised near zero, so the model begins as a pass-through and must earn any departure from the prior. It did not work. The gate converged to a small value ($0.081 \pm 0.007$), but accuracy became markedly less stable ($R^2 = 0.681 \pm 0.237$, with 7 of 25 fold estimates below $0.70$ and one at $-0.24$). Shrinking the gate rescales the gradient reaching the correction head, whose raw output grows to compensate; the product is then poorly controlled on unseen data. A workable variant would need to penalise correction magnitude directly rather than gate it after the fact.

\subsection{On the Structural Layer and the Anisotropy Hypothesis}
Our results do not refute the physical hypothesis that motivated this work --- that in-plane anisotropy shapes band gaps in rectangular 2D lattices \cite{rodin2014strain,tran2014layer,kim2015observation}. They show only that the present implementation cannot test it. The layer was wired to two formation energies rather than to geometric quantities, and the available data contain no in-plane lattice vectors. A genuine test requires $a$ and $b$, which is the single most valuable extension of this work.

\subsection{Implications for Practice}
Three points follow. First, hybrid residual architectures must be validated against their own prior with an out-of-fold construction; compared against an in-sample prior they can appear to help while doing nothing, and the appearance is reproducible enough to survive review. Second, formula-derived indicator features should be treated as identifiers and excluded, since they inflate dimensionality without supporting generalisation. Third, in this data regime the reliable route to accuracy is ensemble diversity rather than architectural coupling: the same five base models that individually plateau near $R^2 \approx 0.86$ combine to $0.877$.

\subsection{Comparison with Published C2DB Results}
Direct numerical comparison with prior C2DB band-gap studies requires care. Reported errors as low as RMSE $0.09$ eV \cite{zhang2021bandgap} are obtained with a semi-local band gap supplied as an input feature, making the task $\Delta$-learning between functionals. Our feature set contains no band gap at any level of theory, and our MAE of $0.363$ eV should be read against results in the $0.24$--$0.53$ eV range reported for descriptor-only settings \cite{zhang2021bandgap,dau2023descriptor}. We consider the leakage-controlled protocol, rather than the absolute error, to be the contribution.

\subsection{Limitations}
All data derive from a single database and a single Bravais class, with 1{,}169 labelled materials. The held-out set of 179 materials limits the resolution of single-split comparisons, as noted in Section~\ref{sec:results}. We compare against tabular baselines only, not against structure-aware graph neural networks \cite{xie2018crystal,schutt2018schnet,chen2019graph,choudhary2021atomistic,chen2022universal}, which require relaxed atomic coordinates that this tabular setting does not supply.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Under a leakage-controlled protocol, a physics-informed hybrid residual architecture does not improve band-gap prediction for two-dimensional materials relative to its own gradient-boosted prior, and none of its physics-motivated components contributes measurably. The improvement reported in an earlier version of this work is attributable to evaluation artefacts, principally an in-sample prior construction that trained the correction head to be inert --- a mechanism our ablation reproduces deliberately.

On the same data, heterogeneous stacked generalisation improves cross-validated $R^2$ from $0.823$ to $0.877$ and MAE from $0.464$ to $0.363$ eV, superior on all 25 fold estimates, with a drop-one ablation showing the two neural members carry the ensemble while the gradient-boosting and support-vector members are redundant. We release the complete pipeline, including every configuration reported here, so that both the negative and the positive result can be reproduced end to end. We regard the negative result as the more useful of the two: hybrid residual designs are widely reported to help, and the specific way in which this one appeared to help is easy to reproduce by accident.
