\title{Leakage-Free Benchmarking of Physics-Informed and Hybrid Residual Models
for Band-Gap Prediction of Two-Dimensional Materials}

\author{Adhvay Jagadeesh, Rutvi Mudalagi, and Marx Akl%
\thanks{The authors are with the Aspiring Scholars Directed Research Program

\begin{abstract}
Hybrid architectures that place a neural residual corrector on top of a
gradient-boosted prior are widely reported to improve tabular property
prediction in materials informatics. We test that claim for HSE06 band-gap
prediction of rectangular-lattice two-dimensional materials under an evaluation
protocol designed to remove the leakage pathways that commonly inflate such
comparisons: composition-grouped cross-validation, fold-local feature encoding
and scaling, out-of-fold construction of the tree prior, a held-out test set
carved before model selection, and significance testing at the fold level with
the Nadeau--Bengio variance correction. On 1{,}169 C2DB materials evaluated over
25 fold estimates from repeated grouped cross-validation, the hybrid residual
model does \emph{not} improve on its own gradient-boosting prior
($R^2 = 0.810 \pm 0.047$ versus $0.823 \pm 0.043$; better on 12 of 25 folds;
$p = 0.27$). A component ablation shows that removing the structural coupling
layer, the boundary penalty, or the anisotropy penalty each leaves accuracy
unchanged or slightly improved, and that the tree prior alone outperforms the
complete framework. Two positive results survive. First, neural models are
competitive with or superior to tree ensembles on this task once
composition-derived descriptors replace formula indicator variables, inverting
the usual tabular-data expectation. Second, heterogeneous stacked generalisation
delivers a large and unambiguous gain over the best single model
($R^2 = 0.877 \pm 0.038$, MAE $= 0.363$ eV, superior on 25 of 25 folds,
$p < 0.001$), and the physics-informed network receives the single largest
non-negative ensemble weight ($0.35$). We conclude that the benefit previously
attributed to physics-informed residual coupling in this setting is not
reproducible under leakage-free evaluation, while ensemble diversity --- to
which the physics-informed model is the largest contributor --- is.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{ccurate} prediction of electronic band gaps ($E_g$) is a
foundational objective in computational materials design. The magnitude and
nature of the band gap govern whether a low-dimensional material acts as a
metal, semiconductor, or insulator, and directly determine suitability for
field-effect transistors, valleytronics, optoelectronic sensors, and
photocatalytic water splitting \cite{raissi2019physics}. Density functional
theory with hybrid functionals such as HSE06 offers excellent accuracy at
computational scaling that renders high-throughput screening impractical, which
motivates fast surrogate estimators.

For heterogeneous tabular materials data, decision-tree ensembles frequently
match or exceed deep architectures \cite{breiman2001random, friedman2001greedy,
shwartzziv2022tabular}, although the conditions under which this holds are
narrower than often assumed \cite{mcelfresh2023when}. A natural response is the
hybrid residual design: a tree supplies a prior estimate and a neural network
learns a correction, optionally regularised by physical constraints
\cite{raissi2019physics}. Such designs are attractive because they appear to
combine the sample efficiency of ensembles with the smoothness of neural
function approximation.

This paper reports a careful test of that design, and the test is largely
negative. Our contribution is threefold.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A leakage-free evaluation protocol} for tabular materials
regression, addressing six specific pathways --- identifier-like features,
whole-dataset encoding, in-sample construction of stacked priors, polymorphs
straddling folds, absent held-out data, and significance tests applied at the
sample rather than fold level. Leakage of exactly these kinds has been
identified as a principal driver of irreproducibility in machine-learning-based
science \cite{kapoor2023leakage}.

\item \textbf{A negative result.} Under that protocol, the physics-informed
hybrid residual architecture does not improve on its own gradient-boosted prior,
and its individual physics-motivated components contribute nothing measurable.
We report the ablation that establishes this, and we diagnose the mechanism.

\item \textbf{A positive alternative.} Heterogeneous stacked generalisation
\cite{wolpert1992stacked, breiman1996stacked} yields a large, significant, and
stable improvement on the same data and protocol, with the physics-informed
network carrying the largest ensemble weight.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Dataset}
We use 1{,}169 rectangular-lattice (\textit{op}) two-dimensional materials from
the Computational 2D Materials Database \cite{haastrup2018c2db} carrying an
HSE06 band-gap label. The target $y \in \mathbb{R}^+$ ranges from $0.001$ to
$8.85$ eV (mean $2.60$ eV).

A note on sample count: an earlier revision of this work reported 930
materials. That figure resulted from applying a blanket missing-value filter
across all columns, which discarded 239 labelled entries because a single
descriptor (total magnetic moment) was absent. We instead impute that column
from training-fold medians and carry an explicit missingness indicator,
recovering the full labelled set.

\subsection{Descriptors}
Each material is represented by seven continuous C2DB descriptors (energy above
hull, heat of formation, thickness, total energy, unit-cell area, vacuum level,
total magnetic moment), three nominal descriptors (magnetic state, layer group,
stoichiometry prototype), an atom count, a packing-density proxy
$\rho_{\mathrm{cell}} = N_{\mathrm{atoms}} / A_{\mathrm{cell}}$, and 120 Magpie
elemental-property statistics \cite{ward2016magpie} computed with matminer
\cite{ward2018matminer}.

Magpie statistics replace the raw chemical formula. A previous revision one-hot
encoded the formula string into 1{,}077 indicator columns, of which 825 occurred
in exactly one material. Such columns are row identifiers rather than
descriptors: a formula appearing only in a validation fold contributes an
all-zero block and cannot generalise.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
Six controls define the protocol.

\emph{Composition grouping.} 176 labelled materials share a chemical formula
with at least one other entry. These are polymorphs, distinguished by layer
group, whose band gaps differ by up to $2.92$ eV. Because composition-derived
descriptors are near-identical within such a pair, an ungrouped split allows a
model to memorise one polymorph and be scored on another. All splits are
therefore \texttt{GroupKFold} on chemical formula.

\emph{Fold-local preprocessing.} Categorical vocabularies, imputation medians,
and standardisation statistics are estimated on the training fold only.

\emph{Out-of-fold priors.} The hybrid's correction head is trained against
out-of-fold predictions from an inner five-fold split, not in-sample
predictions of the fitted tree. This is the standard stacked-generalisation
construction \cite{wolpert1992stacked}; training a corrector against in-sample
predictions exposes it to a prior of far higher quality than it meets at
inference.

\emph{Held-out test set.} 15\% of materials, split by formula group, are
withheld before any model selection.

\emph{Equal tuning budget.} All baselines receive an identical randomised
hyperparameter search within each training fold.

\emph{Repeated cross-validation and corrected inference.} We report five repeats
of grouped five-fold cross-validation, giving 25 fold estimates. Model
comparisons use the Nadeau--Bengio variance-corrected paired $t$-test
\cite{nadeau2003inference}, which accounts for the positive correlation induced
by overlapping training sets. We note that a paired test applied to per-sample
squared errors --- as used in an earlier revision of this work --- treats
non-independent quantities as independent observations of model performance and
overstates significance by roughly two orders of magnitude in effective sample
size.

\subsection{Models}
Baselines are random forest (RF), gradient boosting (GBR), support vector
regression (SVR), a deep multilayer perceptron (MLP), and a physics-informed
network (PINN) sharing the MLP backbone with an added boundary penalty
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phy}} = \mathrm{ReLU}(-\hat{y})
  + 0.1\left(e^{\mathrm{ReLU}(0.05-\hat{y})}-1\right),
\end{equation}
which discourages the physically impossible negative gaps.

The hybrid forms $\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{hybrid}} = [\mathbf{x},\;
\mathbf{x}\odot\hat{y}_{\mathrm{prior}},\;\hat{y}_{\mathrm{prior}}]$ and adds a
learned correction to the prior. A structural coupling layer builds
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{s} = \left[g_1 g_2,\; g_1^2-g_2^2,\;
  \sqrt{g_1^2+g_2^2+\epsilon},\; g_1,\; g_2\right]
\end{equation}
from two geometric channels and projects it back onto the feature map.

An important correction concerns those channels. The earlier implementation
selected them by array position (indices 0 and 1), which in the assembled matrix
were energy above hull and heat of formation --- two formation energies. The
layer's physical motivation therefore described a transform of quantities it did
not receive. Channels are now resolved by name. We emphasise that the available
C2DB export contains no in-plane lattice vectors $a$ and $b$; the geometric
channels are unit-cell area and thickness, and we accordingly describe the layer
as a soft geometric inductive bias rather than as a derivation from elasticity
theory.

The hybrid does not improve on its prior. It is better on 12 of 25 fold

Two baselines invert the expected ordering: the MLP and the PINN both outperform
every tree ensemble. With identifier-like features removed and composition
statistics supplied, this task falls outside the regime in which boosted trees
dominate neural networks \cite{mcelfresh2023when}.

No physics-motivated component contributes positively. Removing the structural
layer, the boundary penalty, or the anisotropy penalty leaves performance
unchanged to within noise, and the tree prior alone is the strongest
configuration in the table.

The second row is diagnostic. Reverting to the leaky in-sample prior
\emph{raises} measured $R^2$. With a prior that appears near-perfect on training
rows, the correction head has almost no residual signal to fit and learns to
emit approximately zero, degenerating into a pass-through of the tree. The
apparent benefit of the original architecture was therefore substantially an
artefact of the head being trained to do nothing.

\subsection{Uncertainty calibration}
The architecture predicts quantiles at $\tau \in \{0.25, 0.50, 0.75\}$, and
earlier revisions described these as uncertainty estimates. The nominal
$q_{0.25}$--$q_{0.75}$ interval should cover 50\% of outcomes; measured
empirical coverage is $17.2\%$ at a mean interval width of $2.47$ eV. The
intervals are simultaneously wide and badly miscalibrated, and we therefore make
no uncertainty claim for this architecture. Proper scoring of predictive
distributions \cite{gneiting2007probabilistic} should accompany any such claim.

\subsection{Stacked generalisation}
A ridge meta-learner over out-of-fold predictions of all five base models
achieves $R^2 = 0.877 \pm 0.038$ and MAE $= 0.363$ eV, superior to GBR on all 25
fold estimates ($p < 0.001$). The non-negative least-squares variant performs
equivalently and is interpretable; its mean weights are PINN $0.350$, MLP
$0.299$, GBR $0.246$, RF $0.127$, SVR $0.000$.

The physics-informed network carries the largest single weight. This is the
defensible form of the claim that physical regularisation helps here: not that a
physics-informed corrector improves a tree prior, but that a physics-informed
network is the most valuable single contributor to a diverse ensemble.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Why residual correction fails}
Once the prior is constructed out of fold, its residuals on unseen materials are
dominated by irreducible noise rather than by recoverable structure. An
unconstrained corrector fitted to such residuals adds variance without removing
bias. This mechanism is not specific to materials data; residual-boosting
correctors are known to produce negative gains when the residual signal is
noise-dominated.

We tested the natural remedy --- a shrinkage gate constraining the correction by
$\sigma(\cdot) \in (0,1)$, initialised near zero so the model begins as a
pass-through and must earn any departure from the prior. It did not work. The
gate converged to a small value ($0.081 \pm 0.007$), but accuracy became
markedly less stable ($R^2 = 0.681 \pm 0.237$, with 7 of 25 folds below $0.70$
and one at $-0.24$). Shrinking the gate rescales the gradient reaching the
correction head, whose raw output grows to compensate; the product is then
poorly controlled on unseen data. A workable version would need to penalise
correction magnitude directly rather than gate it post hoc.

\subsection{Implications}
Three points follow for practitioners. First, hybrid residual architectures
should be validated against their own prior with an out-of-fold construction;
compared against a prior built in sample, they can appear to help while doing
nothing. Second, formula-derived indicator features should be treated as
identifiers and excluded. Third, in this data regime the reliable route to
accuracy is ensemble diversity rather than architectural coupling.

\subsection{Limitations}
All data derive from a single database and a single Bravais class, with 1{,}169
labelled materials. The structural layer operates on unit-cell area and
thickness because in-plane lattice vectors are unavailable in the current
export; recovering $a$ and $b$ would permit a direct test of the anisotropy
hypothesis, which the present work cannot settle. We compare against tabular
baselines only, not against graph neural networks, which require relaxed atomic
coordinates that this tabular setting does not supply.

\section{Conclusion}
Under a leakage-free protocol, a physics-informed hybrid residual architecture
does not improve band-gap prediction for two-dimensional materials relative to
its own gradient-boosted prior, and none of its physics-motivated components
contributes measurably. The improvement reported previously is attributable to
evaluation artefacts, principally an in-sample prior construction that trained
the correction head to be inert. On the same data, heterogeneous stacked
generalisation improves $R^2$ from $0.823$ to $0.877$ and MAE from $0.464$ to
$0.363$ eV, superior on all 25 fold estimates, with the physics-informed network
the largest ensemble contributor. We release the complete pipeline, including
every configuration reported here, so that both the negative and the positive
result can be reproduced end to end.
